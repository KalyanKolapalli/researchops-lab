# ResearchOps Lab — design case study

## Status
Prototype and evaluation plan. No participant research has been conducted. Target-user descriptions and user needs below are hypotheses to validate.

## Problem and context
Plan usability tasks, compare prototype versions and turn anonymous observations into an evidence trail. A student may use this interface on a phone between activities, or on a laptop while planning. Small screens, interruptions and limited attention are anticipated constraints.

## Design rationale
Independent and assisted completion remain separate. Anonymous identifiers reduce unnecessary data collection. A visible fictional-data notice prevents demo observations being mistaken for participant evidence. Version metrics remain descriptive rather than implying a causal A/B experiment. Per-task filters reduce one source of confounding. Consent is a user-entered process checkpoint, not proof that a consent procedure was adequate.

## Interaction flow
Inspect the initial state → adjust a control or complete a labeled form → review immediate feedback → retain or export the result. Empty results provide a next action. Explicit buttons support keyboard use.

## Evaluation plan
Recruit 5 consenting volunteers. Explain that the interface, not the participant, is being evaluated. Collect anonymous IDs only and agree a retention period. Do not collect names, recordings or identifiable personal details without appropriate consent.

Task: Create a task, record one independent and one assisted attempt, and interpret the success metric.

Measure independent completion, completion with assistance, failures, task time, errors and a 1–7 ease rating. Record observed problems separately from interpretations. The ease item is not a SUS questionnaire. A small convenience sample cannot establish population-level findings.

## Accessibility review plan
Check keyboard sequence, focus visibility, form labels, status announcements, contrast, zoom/reflow and screen-reader interpretation. Validate on real mobile devices before claiming mobile usability.

## Reflection prompts
What assumptions failed? Which error cost users most time? Did the revision improve task success? What tradeoff did the change introduce?
